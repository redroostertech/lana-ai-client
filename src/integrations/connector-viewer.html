Understanding the Activity Overview

What is this?

The Activity Overview is a calendar-style chart that shows when your organization was active over the last 90 days. Each small square represents one day; the color shows how much activity happened that day.

Why does it matter?

It helps you spot patterns at a glance: which days of the week are busiest, whether activity is steady or concentrated in certain weeks, and how engagement has changed over time. Use it to understand how your team uses the system and when activity peaks.

How we track activity

Activity is counted from usage across your whole organization. Each time someone in your org uses Lana (for example, starting or replying to conversations, viewing or uploading documents, running searches, or other interactions), that counts as activity. The chart adds up all of these actions per day so you can see when your org is most engaged.

The numbers you see are total activity events for that day, not unique users. So a busy day might show a high count because many people used the system or because a few people were very active.

What am I looking at?

  • Rows. Each row is a day of the week (Sunday at the top through Saturday at the bottom).
  • Columns. Each column is one week. Time runs left to right: older weeks on the left, recent weeks on the right.
  • Colors. Each square’s color shows how much activity happened that day. See “Color scale” below for the exact ranges.

Color scale

The chart uses five levels based on the number of activity events that day:

  • Light gray: No activity (0 events)
  • Light green: Low activity (1 to 4 events)
  • Medium green: Moderate activity (5 to 9 events)
  • Dark green: High activity (10 to 14 events)
  • Darkest green: Very high activity (15 or more events)

The legend under the chart shows “Less” to “More” as a quick reference.

How do I use it?

Interacting with the chart. Hover over any square to see the exact date and activity count for that day. No clicking required; just move your cursor over the chart to explore.

Using the data for your business. Use the heatmap to see when your organization is most engaged and spot trends over time. For example:

  • Compare week to week: are recent weeks busier or quieter than earlier ones?
  • See which days of the week are busiest (e.g. midweek vs Monday/Friday) so you can plan capacity, support, or training.
  • Notice quiet stretches: low or gray activity for many days in a row may mean low adoption, seasonal lulls, or a need to encourage usage.
  • Use the pattern as a baseline: once you know your “normal” activity, you can tell when something changes (e.g. after a rollout or a process change).

The chart doesn’t tell you why activity went up or down, but it shows you when it did so you can dig in elsewhere (e.g. reports or conversations) to understand the cause.
